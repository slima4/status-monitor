Read the date, then check your renewal

This tool reads the expiration event in the domain's RDAP registration data. It looks up the registered name: shop.example.co.uk becomes example.co.uk. The result names the domain it checked and links to the registry response.

fieldhow to use it
expiry dateThe public registration expiration timestamp, shown in UTC. Confirm your payment deadline with the registrar.
days remainingWhole days until that timestamp. Less than a day is shown explicitly; a passed date is never displayed as time remaining.
registrarThe registrar named in the response, when available. Use your registrar account to check billing and auto-renewal.
checked atWhen we fetched the record. Results may be reused for up to 15 minutes to reduce requests to registries.

A future date is not a payment receipt. Registry auto-renewal can move the public expiry date while a registrar still needs your renewal payment. If the dates disagree, ask your registrar.

An unavailable date is not an available domain

Some domain endings do not expose expiry through RDAP. Registries can also throttle requests or temporarily stop answering. This checker reports those limits instead of guessing a date or claiming the domain is available to register. It does not fall back to WHOIS.

Expiration does not mean immediate deletion. Recovery rules vary; ICANN's renewal and expiration FAQ explains the distinction for registrants. If a renewal is overdue, contact your registrar rather than waiting for the website to stop loading.

Watch registration separately from the website

A parking page can answer a status-code check after a registration lapses. That failure is covered in why your domain can expire while your uptime monitor stays green. A registration check and an HTTP check answer different questions.

The certificate has its own expiry date. Read it with the SSL certificate checker. If the name points somewhere unexpected, compare its records with the DNS lookup tool.
